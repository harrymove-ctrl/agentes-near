## Context

Presence was one boolean: a person with a WebSocket open was "online". People asked for the usual chat statuses (online, busy, hidden, offline) and a line saying what they are doing. In a workspace where agents work next to people, the same questions apply to an agent: is it on heavy work right now, answering someone, or idle?

## Decision

1. **People choose an availability**, per organization (`organization_members.availability`): `auto` (online while connected), `away` (out for a while: answers may take time), `busy` (online, but rather not interrupted) or `hidden`. What others see (`presence` on `GET …/people`) is `online`, `away`, `busy` or `offline`; a hidden person is `offline` to everyone else and `hidden` only to themselves, so the two can't be told apart. While hidden, `last_seen_at` stops moving and no connect/disconnect event goes out for them: either would give them away. `GET …/members` returns neither field.
2. **A written status** (`status_text`, at most 100 characters, an optional emoji and an optional end, `status_until`). An expired one reads as none: it's cleared on read, no job. `PUT …/me/status` sets either part; empty text clears the line.
3. **Agents' presence comes from their work**, never set by hand (`GET …/agent-status`): `busy` on a routine, a task (`issue_id`) or a run going for over 2 minutes, or when the agent said so; `online` while answering or waiting on an answer, and for 30 minutes after its last run; `away` after that; `offline` when held by the operators. An agent with no AI is drawn offline by the apps, which already read `ai-status`.
4. **Agents write their own line** with the built-in `set_status` (text, emoji, `busy`, optional `minutes`): "Revisando o PR #12". Without `minutes` it is tied to the run that wrote it (`agents.status_run_id`) and is gone when that run ends; with them it lasts up to a day. It is in every run's core tools (no scope), changes nothing but the agent's own line, and the run policy allows it for every kind of run (kind `self`), unaudited like `pass`.
5. **Agents see each other's status** in `find_agent` ("@ops: Ops (busy, on heavy work: Deploy da v2)"), which used to say only "working now". Nothing routes by it yet.
6. **Live.** Changes go out as org broadcasts with ids only: `member.status` (a person changed their status, or their first connection opened / last one closed) and `agent.status` (an agent's line). Clients refetch the people or the agents' presence; the agents' list also polls every 30 seconds, since presence follows time and runs elsewhere in the workspace. A person's account button reads their own row, as every list does. The app's first read of the people often reaches the server before it counts the app's socket, so it says offline; the `member.status` that counting sends corrects it, and the apps' query client (`LiveQueryClient`) reads a list again when its invalidation lands during its first read, which TanStack Query would otherwise drop. A reconnect reads the people again, since what was announced while the socket was down never reached it.

## How an agent's status is set, and why it never sticks

7. **Three sources, one slot.** An agent's line comes from, in order: its own words (`set_status`, `agents.status_source = 'self'`), a line picked for the run (`'auto'`, §8), or nothing. An automatic line never replaces the agent's own words while they hold, nor another live run's task, mission or routine line, and a run with nothing to say never clears another live run's line. Both are tied to the run that wrote them (`status_run_id`); `minutes` keeps an own line at least that long, past the run's end.
8. **Jev picks the line, it never writes one.** Jev answers typed questions; it doesn't generate text. So code builds the candidate lines from facts everyone in the workspace may see, and Jev chooses one (a `choice` with a `none` option) in a request of its own (`askRunStatus`), sent beside the run as it starts and never in its way: the run doesn't wait for it, and a slow or failed answer only costs the line (it used to ride on the tool-group request, where a bigger request put the run's tools at risk). Candidates, each with its emoji, written in the workspace's language from templates:
   - the task it works on (`issue_id`: "Trabalhando em: Login com Google"), the mission, the routine ("Rotina: Relatório semanal"); a task's or mission's title only when its channel is public (a task with no channel is the workspace's);
   - where it answers, only a public channel ("Respondendo no #campanhas"); a DM or a private channel gives no line (a status is seen by the whole workspace: it never carries a private name or text, and "in a private conversation" tells nobody anything);
   - what it does, from the tool groups it may use ("Pesquisando na web", "Programando", "Lendo o Drive", "Analisando dados", "Escrevendo na wiki").
   The trigger message goes into Jev's state only (untrusted, never shown). **A line has to be worth showing**: each candidate also gets a `noul` ("would the team learn something useful from it next to the agent's name?"), and the chosen one shows only at 0.5 or more; a quick reply or small talk gets no line. At most 8 candidates. `none`, not worth it, or no Jev (a self-host without a TypeSafe key): no line, except what the work is for (task, mission, routine), which always is.
9. **Busy from the start.** In the same request (`statusQuestions`, `askRunStatus`, applied by `startRunStatus`), a `score` question rates how heavy the work is (a quick answer · a few short steps · minutes of work · long work in the background). Two top levels with probability ≥ 0.6 mark the run busy at once, instead of after 2 minutes. Without Jev, the 2-minute rule (§3) alone. The judge gets `score` as its third question type (`judge.ts`; the LLM fallback answers a level).
10. **It moves with the work.** When a run starts a step in another tool group, the automatic line follows it (code only, at most once every 30 s), only for real work: a run Jev rated heavy, a routine, or one going for over 2 minutes (a quick reply that opens one page gets no line). An agent's own line is checked then: a `noul` ("does the line still describe the step it is on?"); under 0.3, the automatic line takes its place. Waiting on an approval, an answer or its computer, the line becomes "Esperando aprovação" / "Esperando resposta" / "Esperando o computador", and the agent reads `online`, not busy.
11. **Nothing can stick.** Every line is checked when read, never cleaned up by a job:
   - a run-bound line shows only while its run is active, and a `running` run counts only with a heartbeat from the last 90 s (a dead worker's run stops counting at once; recovery fails or requeues it within a minute, `recovery.ts`);
   - a run waiting for more than a day loses its line (it stays `online`);
   - an end time (`minutes`) is at most a day, and a person's `until` is checked on read;
   - every run start and end sends `agent.status`, so the apps refresh at once; their 30 s poll is only the backstop.

## Consequences

- Presence is still ephemeral (the `Presence` adapter); only the choice and the line are stored.
- Jev calls: one per run start (`agent_status`: a choice, a score and a worth `noul` per candidate, at most 8), off the run's path. Line changes inside a run are code only, beside the tool call; the staleness `noul` runs only when an agent wrote its own line and the run changes group. `agent.status` goes out on a run's start and end and when a line actually changes.
- Open questions, left for later: whether `busy` should hold a person's pushes (Do not disturb), an automatic `away` for people idle while connected, a person's line from their calendar ("Em reunião até 15h"), and whether the router should prefer an agent that isn't busy.

## Addendum (2026-10-31): a run reads how its teammates are now

Agents told people that two teammates still had no AI hours after they got one: the conversation keeps the "I can't answer yet: I have no AI connected" cards, and nothing in a run's context said otherwise. A run whose context names other agents ("Where you are", "# Your team") now gets "# Your teammates now" (`teammatesNowPrompt`), one line per agent in the context's order: "no AI connected yet" or "its computer is away for now" (`agentAiStates`), "paused" when held, busy with what (its line), or else "has an AI, answers when called", and that this outweighs what older messages say. Idle is no state to report for an agent: a mention or `ask_agent` wakes it at once, and "away" read as "not available" (`find_agent` still says presence).

It is a per-turn block, read whole (`found`): after "Where you are" it would be the first thing a computer brain's "# This turn" clips. A run that resumes (its computer back, a decision, an answer) reads it again (`teammatesNowAgain`, from the agents the block keeps in `ContextBlock.teammates`), since a wait for a sleeping computer can last the night; within one stretch of work it stays as read, so the model brain's prompt cache holds. Only the agents the context names (the conversation's, and "# Your team" up to `TEAM_IN_PROMPT`): one AI check each per run and per resume.

## Addendum (2026-11-03): out of office

12. **An agent with nothing to think with is out of office.** An agent whose plan (Claude Code, Codex…) hit its limit with no model to fall back on (the fallback off, or no key for its model), or whose monthly budget for the model is spent, reads `out` instead of online or away (`out-of-office.ts`, from what is already stored: the runner's plan readings, `machine_accounts.usage` / `executors.ownUsage`, this process's limits, and this month's `agent_budget_periods`). Its line says why and when it is back: `out_of_plan` with the plan window's reset (null when the plan didn't say), `out_of_budget` with the next month ("Sem limite no plano · volta às 15:00"). Its own words (`set_status`) stay; held still reads offline; a run of its own that is actually thinking (a fallback that works) keeps it online or busy. The badge is the agents' body in the info color with an arrow out the door.
13. **The router leaves it out.** A message nobody mentioned it in goes to the others (`RouterDeps.outOfOffice`, `outOfOfficeIds`): it would only answer after the reset, late and out of context. A mention or a DM still reaches it and waits for the reset as before (ADR 0118). Its teammates read it too: `find_agent` and "# Your teammates now" say "out of office: … back at …", so they ask someone else meanwhile.
14. **People can be out of office too.** A person picks "Out of office" (`availability = 'out'`, beside away, busy and hidden; the "Vacation" suggestion now picks it). Unlike away and busy it shows whether they are connected or not, since that is its point, and it ends with their line's `until` ("back Monday"): after it they read as `auto` again (`availabilityNow`, checked on read). Same badge as an agent out of office. No migration: `availability` is text.

## Addendum (2026-11-09): new agents and the right hand

15. **Away is for an agent idle a while since it last worked.** One that never worked counts from its making: online for
    30 minutes after it was made, then away, instead of away from the start. The team's right hand (ADR 0137) is never
    away: it is where the workspace's people come to talk, so it reads online unless busy, out, held or with no AI.
