# 0085. Workspace roles: roles that add up

- Status: Accepted (built: the server, the shared client, the web and the phone app; channel and agent exceptions, and guests, come next). Amended 2026-11-04: §3 and §13 (`use_others_plans`); 2026-11-05: §3 and §14 (`mention_everyone`)
- Date: 2026-10-30
- Revises: ADR 0006 (the fixed owner / admin / member table)
- Code: `packages/engine/src/permissions.ts`, `packages/engine/src/audience.ts` (addendum), `packages/engine/src/roles.ts`, `packages/engine/src/organizations.ts` (`Membership`, `can`, `outranks`), `packages/db/src/schema/tenancy.ts` (`org_roles`, `organization_member_roles`), migrations `0130_roles` and `0211_own_computers`, `apps/server/src/routes/app/roles.ts`, `packages/client/src/lib/permissions.ts`, `packages/client/src/lib/roles.ts`, `packages/ui/src/components/RoleMark.tsx`, `apps/web/src/components/roles/RolesSection.tsx`

## Context

A workspace had three fixed roles. "Admin" meant everything but ownership, "member" meant chatting; nothing in between. A team that wanted someone to approve deploys without editing agents, or to see costs without managing people, had to make them an admin. The checks themselves had drifted: "admin" was `manage_members` in missions and threads, `manage_agents` in approvals and computers, `configure` in wiki locks and memory, and a few places compared the role in SQL.

## Decision

1. **Roles add up.** A workspace has any number of roles (`org_roles`); a person holds any number (`organization_member_roles`) and may do what any of them allows. Every member also holds the workspace's base role, `everyone` ("Todos" in the apps), without a row: it is what any member may do. Each role has a name (at most 40), one of the role icons, a color from the agents' palette but sky (sky stays the agents' own), its keys, and two switches the apps don't use yet (`mentionable`, `hoist`).
2. **Owner is not a role.** `organization_members.role` only says whether the person owns the workspace (`owner` / `member`). An owner may do everything, including what no role grants: ownership itself and deleting the workspace (`manage_owners`). A workspace keeps at least one owner, as before.
3. **Keys, in five areas** (`PERMISSION_AREAS`): conversations (`post`, `create_channels`, since 2026-11-05 `mention_everyone`, `manage_channels`), agents (`talk_to_agents`, `assign_work`, `manage_routines`, `approve_actions`, `create_agents`, `manage_agents`), apps and computers (`pair_computers`, the four computer keys of §12, `manage_connectors`, `manage_credentials`, and since 2026-11-04 `use_others_plans`), wiki and files (`edit_wiki`, `upload_files`, `review_memory`) and the workspace (`invite_people`, `view_usage`, `view_audit`, `configure`, `manage_members`, `manage_roles`, `manage_billing`). `administrator` grants every key. `read` is any member's. Each area has a "use" set (its everyday keys); the apps offer an area at three levels: like `everyone`, use, manage (every key), or item by item.
4. **One check.** The server resolves a person's keys with their membership (`getMembership`: their roles' keys and `everyone`'s, in one query) and every check is `can(membership, key)` / `requirePermission`. Where a statement has to pick people (who may approve, who hears about a computer, who drives an agent's desktop), `maySql` / `membersWhoMaySql` ask the same thing in SQL; nothing compares role names any more. The apps get the resolved keys as `permissions` on each workspace (`GET /me`, `GET /organizations/{id}`) and only hide what isn't there.
5. **Where each key lands** (the actions the app already had): approving an agent's action needs `approve_actions` (or owning the agent, as before); driving an agent's desktop or browser `control_computers` (*amended 2026-11-04:* the browser alone `control_browsers`, and a person's own agents by `everyone`'s level, §12); pairing a computer `pair_computers`; creating agents `create_agents`, each its creator's own (another owner or none, giving it apps and mailboxes, and editing any agent stay `manage_agents`); where an agent works, its owner or `manage_agents`, only in channels they're in and may `post` in, or manage (`manage_channels` or having created the channel): one check (`placesAgentsIn`) for creating it, its page and a channel's members, an agent's tool and imports among them, while who manages a channel still takes any agent out of it; routines `manage_routines` (or owning the agent); renaming, archiving and arranging channels and categories `manage_channels` (or having created the channel); overriding someone's thread `manage_channels`, someone's mission `manage_agents`; starting a mission `assign_work`; writing the wiki `edit_wiki`; uploading to the Drive `upload_files`; deciding memory and proposals `review_memory`; invitations and requests to join `invite_people`; the audit log `view_audit`. The agent's audience rule (`canTalkTo`, `audienceSql`) also needs `talk_to_agents`, except for the agent's own owner: one place, so DMs, mentions, the router, missions and routines follow it.
6. **Order is hierarchy.** `position` orders the roles, 0 the highest; `everyone` is always below them all. A person's rank is their highest role's position. Anyone but an owner only changes, deletes, moves or gives roles below their own rank, and only changes the roles of people below them (themselves aside). An owner's roles only owners change.
7. **Nobody gives what they can't do.** Creating or changing a role, anyone but an owner may only add or remove keys they hold themselves; giving a role (to someone else, to themselves, in an invitation or an approved request), only one whose keys they all hold (`role.grant_forbidden`). `everyone` never holds `administrator`.
8. **Invitations carry roles.** An email invitation and an approved request to join carry the roles the person gets on joining (`role_ids`, checked against the sender's rank); roles deleted since are skipped.
9. **Every workspace starts with two roles**: `everyone` with what a member could do before (send messages, create channels, talk to agents, give missions, pair computers, edit the wiki, upload files) and "Admin" with `administrator`. Migration 0130 gave them to every workspace and moved its admins to "Admin".
10. **On screen** (Settings › Papéis e permissões): the roles as rows, each summed up in one sentence (its keys past `everyone`'s); the owner shown on top as a role. A role opens on its own page: its mark (the role's color in the agents' dither, the people tile's shape), the administrator switch, each area as a segmented control whose options carry the tool-review level swatches, item by item behind "Ajustar" (keys that come from `everyone` or from administrator show it and can't be turned off there), the people who hold it, and its name, color and icon. People and invitations show each person's roles as chips.
11. **The operator API keeps its words.** Its `role` is `owner`, `admin` (holds `administrator`) or `member`; its admins list is the owners and the administrators.
12. **The agents' computers, one choice per role** (*added 2026-11-04*). What a role reaches of the agents' computers is one of three levels, not loose switches: none (not even the browser view or a sign-in request), the browser only (the agent's browser view, taking it over, opening a page, its sign-in requests and browser profiles, never the full screen or the terminal), or the whole computer (its full screen, taking control of it, the terminal, and the browser). Four keys hold it, each giving the ones below it (`IMPLIED` in `permissions.ts`, spread by `resolvePermissions`, `roleGrants` and `maySql`, and by `grants` in the apps): `control_computers` and `control_browsers` on every agent of the workspace, held by the other roles; `own_computers` and `own_browsers` on what is a person's own (an agent they are responsible for, a computer they connected, a run they asked for and a request addressed to them), held by `everyone`, so a role adds every agent and `everyone` bounds what each person reaches of their own. Who reaches an agent is as before; the level is checked on top, in one place each: `screenAccess`/`requireBrowserAccess` and `requireBrowserControl` (watching and driving either screen, so the HTTP routes, `browser.watch` on `/api/ws` and the desktop and terminal sockets), `driveRefusal` (taking control, the terminal, who a desktop request goes to: a new refusal, `role`), `browserHandoffRecipient` and `notifyBrowserHandoff` (a browser request goes and is told only to someone whose roles give the browser), and the browser profiles (`control_browsers` with `manage_connectors`; an agent's owner watching one needs `own_browsers`). `GET …/desktop` answers whoever may see the browser but not the whole computer with a screen that can't be shown (`reason: "browser_only"`, `controlRefusal: "role"`), so the apps show the browser with a line that says why. The workspace's owner holds every key. Defaults: administrator roles hold them all (the whole computer), `everyone` holds `own_computers` (`EVERYONE_DEFAULTS`), and migration 0211 gave `everyone` that in every workspace, which is what any member could do before; roles holding `control_computers` keep it. On the roles screen the tools area shows it as one segmented choice ("Nenhum", "Só o navegador", "Computador inteiro") with a line that says the reach (every agent, or a person's own for Todos); below what Todos gives isn't offered, an administrator role shows it fixed, the tools' "use" level no longer includes `control_computers`, and the four keys aren't listed item by item. The phone app has no roles screen; both apps hide or explain what a person's level doesn't give (the request's buttons, the computer's entry, "Seu papel…").

13. **Other people's plans** (*added 2026-11-04*). `use_others_plans`, "Usar planos de outras pessoas" ("Give the agents they can edit a plan someone else connected. Its owner gets an email and can take it back."), in the apps and computers area: whoever holds it gives any agent they may manage a plan a teammate connected (ADR 0059 §3, amended 2026-11-04). `administrator` grants it, so the "Admin" role and the owners hold it and no migration is needed; `everyone` never does by default, and a role gets it on that area's "manage" level or on its own switch.

14. **Mention everyone** (*added 2026-11-05*). `mention_everyone`, "Mencionar todos" (Discord's "Mention @everyone"), in the conversations area: `@todos` in any conversation but a DM mentions every member. Each language types and reads its own word (`EVERYONE_WORD` in `packages/i18n/src/everyone.ts`: `@everyone`, `@todos`, `@tous`, `@alle`, `@全員`…), any of them is recognised, and the message stores only `meta.mentionsEveryone` (written by `insertMessageWithRuns` alone, never from an agent's message or a caller's meta), so each reader sees the pill in their language. Every person member is mentioned (`mentioned_user_ids`: notification and push as for a mention); every agent member that answers that person there (its audience, the channel's `talk_to_agents`) gets a mention run, never Lodi, and may stay quiet unless also named (`meta.everyoneAgentIds`, `byEveryone` in `reply-policy.ts`). `administrator` grants it, so the "Admin" role and the owners hold it and no migration is needed; `everyone` doesn't. Without it, the "@" lists (web and phone) don't offer it and the word stays plain text.

## Consequences

- No role names in checks: a new action picks a key, and the role screen shows it.
- Every request reads the person's keys with their membership (two indexed subqueries).
- Next: exceptions per channel (who sees, who writes, allow / inherit / deny per role or person; who writes, calls agents and manages is ADR 0134), who approves an agent by role (the audience by role is the addendum below), guests (a base that replaces `everyone` and sees only where they are put, which every read path has to honor), `@role` mentions and `hoist`.

## Addendum (2026-11-01): an agent's audience by role

A workspace uses its roles like departments, and "Só algumas pessoas" took people one at a time.

1. **Roles next to people.** `agents.audience_role_ids` (migration `0163_agent_audience_roles`) sits beside `audience_user_ids`. Under `people` an agent answers its owner, the people named, and whoever holds one of its roles at the time: joining or leaving a role changes access at once, with no change to the agent. Under `owner` both lists wait for it to be let out again (ADR 0059 §5); `everyone` clears both.
2. **One rule.** `canTalkTo` takes the person as a `Speaker` (`talk_to_agents` and their role ids: `speakerFor`, or `speakerOf(membership)`; `Membership` now carries `roleIds`), and `audienceSql` asks the same thing over `organization_member_roles`. Mentions, the router, DMs, dispatch, missions, routines, issues, mailboxes, consultations, the team screen and the agents list's `canTalk` all go through one of the two; a test checks they agree for every audience, owner and person.
3. **What can be picked.** The workspace's own custom roles, at most 100; `everyone` is the "everyone" mode, not a role. Picking a role widens the audience; on a plan its owner or an admin adds one (*amended 2026-11-03*, ADR 0059 §5; `planSharing` is gone). An app that sends no `roleIds` (older phones adding a person from a notice) keeps the roles already picked. A deleted role simply stops matching anyone.
4. **Saying it.** The notice and the error name the roles first (`params.roleIds`, `params.names`: "Dev only replies to Suporte and Ana."), and so does the summary on the agent's page ("Suporte, Tecnologia e mais 2 pessoas").
5. **On screen** (web, Quem pode falar): "Só algumas pessoas ou papéis" opens one picker with a "Papéis" group (each role's mark and how many hold it) above "Pessoas", one search for both, picked roles and people as tokens. The phone app doesn't edit the audience; its "let them in" from a notice keeps the roles.

## Addendum (2026-11-01): other agents can consult it

A front-desk agent that people may talk to orchestrates specialists they may not. Access followed the person behind the request, so the front desk's `ask_agent` to a specialist was refused for a person outside the specialist's audience, and the person read that the specialist's access wasn't open to them.

1. **The switch.** Each agent's "Quem pode falar com o <nome>" card has "Outros agentes podem consultar o <nome>" / "Other agents can consult <name>", on by default (`agents.consultable`, migration `0164_agent_consultable`). It shows when the audience leaves someone out (not under "Todos do workspace"). It is part of the audience (`AgentAudience.consultable`, absent = keep), changed by the agent's owner or an admin, audited as `agent.audience_changed`. On the web it sits under the three choices; on the phone, in "Editar agente".
2. **One rule.** `canTalkTo` takes a `viaAgent` speaker (`viaAgent(speaker)`): asked by another agent for a person, an agent that takes consultations (`takesConsultations`) is reachable even outside its audience. The person still needs `talk_to_agents`, and with nobody behind the run (a routine whose creator left, an unattended run) nobody's reach widens. The agent-side paths use it: `ask_agent` and `tell_agent` (`reachableAgent`), an agent's @mention of an agent outside the conversation or of a member it couldn't wake (`settleReach`, `reachWillHand`), the team in the prompt, `find_agent` and the closest-handle hints. Every person-side path (a person's message, DM, router pick, routine, issue, mailbox, the agents list's `canTalk`) keeps the direct rule, and `audienceSql` stays the person's rule: mentions in the agents' DM wake nobody, so no SQL path needs the agent one.
3. **It answers the agent, never the person.** A consultation runs in the two agents' DM as before. A member an agent @mentioned for such a person isn't woken in that conversation: it gets the mention as a hand-off note in the agents' DM (`meta.reach`: reason `audience` with a `consultationId`, "Consultei a Nina em particular: ela responde a mim, não direto aqui"), and the author gets no "wasn't woken" notice. Its run answers agents only (`answersAgentsOnly`): `consultationOrigin` returns nothing for it, so its approval cards, questions and secret requests stay in the agents' DM instead of the person's conversation (`ask_user` there is refused), and `send_dm` and `post_message` are refused. Its connectors, mailboxes and files act under its own scopes, as any consulted run's.
4. **Approvals.** Its sensitive actions still ask: the request goes to whoever decides for it (its owner, or whoever may approve actions, `approverIds`), never to the person it was consulted for, whose conversation doesn't get the card.
5. **The plan gate holds.** An agent thinking with its owner's personal plan (Claude Code, Codex) answers others only once its owner turns on "Replies to everyone" (ADR 0059 §5): under `owner` it takes no consultation for anyone else, switch or not. Turning the switch back on widens who it serves: its owner or an admin does it (*amended 2026-11-03*, ADR 0059 §5; before, only its owner, through `planSharing`, now gone).
6. **Off.** Today's behavior: refused, and the asking agent reads that the agent works only with some people and takes no questions from other agents for anyone else, and that its owner or an admin can let them in or turn the switch on.
7. **Nothing of it reaches that person.** The run consulted for someone outside its agent's audience lends them nothing: they don't watch its browser while it runs, and `browser_ask_person` asks its owner, not them. Where they read (the asking conversation's consultation list, its side panel, the `consult_reply` notice) they see the consultation's state, never its answer, progress or DM messages, and its reach line opens no panel; the front desk passes on what matters in its own words. A routine's run consulting it for a creator outside the audience keeps its cards in the agents' DM too, not where the routine reports. Saving the switch while the mode stays "only its owner" keeps the people and roles a plan kept out. On a plan answering only its owner the switch isn't offered (it means nothing there). Only `ask_agent` and `tell_agent` suggest consult-only agents for an unknown handle.
